Calculus studies two big questions.

The first is how fast something is changing at a single moment. A car's speedometer answers that kind of question. The part of calculus about change is called differential calculus, and its main tool is the derivative.

The second is how much something adds up. The distance a car travels is its speed added up over time. The part of calculus about adding up is called integral calculus, and its main tool is the integral.

Those two ideas undo each other. A derivative undoes an integral, and an integral undoes a derivative. That fact is called the fundamental theorem of calculus. You do not need to use it yet. You only need to know that the subject is about change and accumulation, and that both rest on a quieter idea called a limit.
